# Work and Career, and Money: catalogue additions

Group: work-money. Prepared 7 October 2026. The machine-readable version is `work-money.json` in this folder.

## Summary

Eleven subcategories now each hold at least three workbooks. Six were already full from the demo catalogue. Five had none: Words That Land, Welcoming Workplaces, Wider Reach, Paying It Down and Patient Investing. This file adds 16 workbooks: 15 to fill those five, and one public-domain classic (Smiles' Thrift) as a fourth title for Money for Later.

New authors: 10. Three are real public-domain authors (William Strunk Jr., Claude C. Hopkins, Charles Mackay). Seven are invented demo authors. Two existing authors are reused: Samuel Smiles (AU-HX8HW) and Tama Rāwiri-Hughes (AU-1ZS6X). One new demo imprint: Brackenfold Books, Leeds (PB-7AFQE), for the UK money titles.

Lengths across the 16: five at 4 weeks, four at 6 weeks, four at 8 weeks, three at 12 weeks.

All new codes are provisional. They were minted with the catalogue's code_rule, using the workbook id, author slug or imprint slug as the key, and checked against every AK-, AU- and PB- code already in the repo.

## Public-domain reasoning

Each classic meets the Tier A test used for the existing five: first published before 1931, and every author died before 1946 (Mackay 1889, Hopkins 1932, Strunk 1946, Smiles 1904). Strunk sits on the line. He died in 1946, so the UK term ran to 31 December 2016, and the text is clear in the UK from 2017. The Gutenberg text is the 1920 Harcourt edition, not the 1959 Strunk and White edition, which is in copyright.

Each still needs a house record in `docs/public-domain/` and a signed review. Canadian, Australian and New Zealand rules are not yet recorded for any classic.

Scientific Advertising has no Project Gutenberg text. A scan of the 1923 Lord and Thomas printing has to be found and transcribed. Modern reprints, including the Dover and Library of Congress facsimile editions, must not be copied, because of the UK typographical arrangement right.

Ruled out:

- George S. Clason, The Richest Man in Babylon. Clason died on 7 April 1957 (Wikipedia). The UK term runs to 31 December 2027, so it is not public domain in the UK until 1 January 2028. Its framing also leans on wealth promises. Not used.
- Dale Carnegie. The brief rules him out. For the record: The Art of Public Speaking (1915, with J. Berg Esenwein) may now be public domain in both markets, because Carnegie died in 1955 and Esenwein in 1946. It is not used here. Legal could look at it later [Crent].
- Wallace Wattles. Not used, because of his money claims.

## Safety and advice notes

- Every Money title carries `not_financial_advice`. This is the value the schema stores. The existing catalogue writes `money_guidance_not_advice`, which the schema maps to the same value. Reader copy: "This workbook is for learning. It is not financial advice."
- No title recommends a product, provider, lender, fund or tax position. Investing titles explain how things work, use made-up numbers for examples, point to the FCA register and suggest a regulated adviser or free guidance. No title promises returns. There are no trading or crypto titles.
- Mackay's bubbles chapters are taught as history and as a caution about hype. Week 3 says plainly that historians now question parts of his tulip account.
- Paying It Down is the one standard-tier Theme here. Its three titles have `safety_profile: standard`, Help now on, and a new signpost `money_worries_lines`. That signpost lists free services as signposts only: MoneyHelper, StepChange, National Debtline and Citizens Advice in the UK, and MABS in Ireland. Akana claims no endorsement or partnership. Numbers and hours still need verifying before they go into `support_lines.json`. Other markets are still to research [check].
- The catalogue has no `standard` value for finance yet. Its only standard value is `wellbeing_standard`. Crent or the Clinical Safety Lead should confirm `standard` is right for debt titles [Crent].
- Two debt titles are marked not group-suitable: Everything I Owe, On One Page and Opening the Brown Envelopes. Both ask for personal figures and letters, which people may not want to bring to a workplace group, even with private answers. The Repayment Notebook is marked group-suitable for facilitated money-wellbeing groups. The Theme itself stays group-suitable, as the taxonomy says.
- Office Days, Home Days and A Newsletter People Look Forward To carry `not_legal_or_tax_advice`, for employment rules and email permission rules.
