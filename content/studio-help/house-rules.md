# House rules

Every Akana workbook follows the same few rules. They protect readers, and they protect you. Our automatic checks look for them, and an editor reads every workbook too.

## No health promises

A workbook is a guide to practise with. It is not treatment. So a workbook never says it treats, cures, heals or diagnoses anything, and it never tells a reader what condition they have.

You can say what an exercise is. You can say it is not a diagnosis or a treatment. You cannot say what it will do for someone's health.

## No claims about how well it works

We do not say an exercise is proven, that research shows it works, that most people find it helps, or put a percentage on it. This applies in full to wellbeing workbooks. In other genres, everyday words like "improve" are fine, but "proven", "scientifically" and percentages are not.

Why: every reader is different. A claim that holds for one study does not hold for the person reading at 2am. We would rather say plainly what to do and let the reader find out.

## No promises about money

Finance, business and career workbooks do not promise income, returns or results. Phrases like "passive income", "double your income" or "guaranteed returns" are flagged. These workbooks carry a note that they are guidance, not financial advice.

## No scores or labels

Wellbeing self-checks are not scored. A reader's answers are never added up into a number, a band or a label. A score can read like a verdict on a person, and we will not do that to a reader.

## Safety lines and Help now

Wellbeing workbooks show Help now on every screen, with free support lines for where the reader lives. The safety lines are written and checked by Akana. Authors cannot change them, and they look the same in every workbook, so a reader in trouble always finds help in the same place.

## Your bio

Your public bio goes through the same claims check, because it sits next to workbooks in every genre. If a phrase is flagged, an editor reads it and may ask you to change it. Your bio shows on your author page only after an editor approves it.

## House style

UK or US spelling, to match your book. No em dashes, no emojis, plain straight quotes. Short sentences. The checks point out each one, so you do not have to hunt.
